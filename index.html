What We Built

A self-updating prediction system that tells mine engineers the expected ground vibration from a blast — before it is fired — using a combination of the proven physics law and machine learning trained on real field data from Bhanegaon mine.

Three Objectives
  • Build an ML model for PPV prediction from one month of field blast data
  • Generate synthetic data to simulate different geological conditions the mine will encounter
  • Make the system autonomous — it detects when its accuracy drops and retrains itself
Overall System Workflow
1
Field Data Collection
44 blast records from Bhanegaon mine — distance, charge, holes, depth, PPV measured at 5 monitoring points per blast
2
Feature Engineering + Synthetic Data
Compute scaled distance, total charge, log-transforms · Generate 600 synthetic records for 3 geological zones → 644 training records total
3
Hybrid Model Training
Physics law gives baseline · Gradient Boosting learns the geological correction · 9 models compared · Best selected
4
Web App Deployment
Streamlit app — enter blast design → get prediction instantly · New records auto-saved to Google Sheets
5
Autonomous Retraining
EWMA monitor tracks prediction error · Triggers automatic retraining when geology changes · No human intervention

How We Created Synthetic Training Data
Expanding 44 real records to 644 — simulating three geological zones the mine will encounter
Why Synthetic Data Was Needed
  • 44 real records is too few to train a reliable ML model alone
  • ML needs hundreds of examples to learn patterns without overfitting
  • Real blasts take months to collect — synthetic fills the gap immediately
  • Published literature validates this approach (Monjezi et al., 2011; Hao & Wu, 2005)
Generation Method — 4 Steps
  • Take a real blast record as the base
  • Add small controlled variation to blast design parameters
  • Calculate PPV using physics law + 16% geological noise
  • Repeat for 600 records across 3 geological zones
PPVsyn = kzone × SD−1.4 × ε  ·  ε ~ N(1.0, 0.16) noise calibrated from Hao & Wu (2005) field measurement uncertainty

Why Physics as the Foundation?
  • The USBM law has been validated over 60 years and thousands of blasts worldwide
  • Guarantees physically consistent predictions — PPV always decreases with distance
  • Engineers and regulators can verify it without needing ML knowledge
  • Provides stable baseline even in areas with no historical data
Why Gradient Boosting for the Correction?
  • Builds trees sequentially — each one specifically corrects previous errors
  • Learns what physics misses: blast scale, seam type, depth interactions
  • No. of Holes is the top feature — the USBM formula ignores it entirely
  • GridSearchCV tuned: 200 trees · learning rate 0.03 · depth 5

Conclusions & Deployment Readiness
A complete, deployable, self-updating solution — built for Indian opencast mining conditions
Three Objectives — All Met
  • Objective 1: Hybrid GBR model trained on real Bhanegaon data. Train R²=0.944, Test R²=0.647, MAE=0.589 mm/s — best RMSE among all 9 models tested
  • Objective 2: 600 synthetic records across 3 geological zones. Validates model performance under conditions the mine will face as it excavates deeper
  • Objective 3: EWMA autonomous drift detection — model watches its own error and retrains on latest 100 records when geology changes. Zero human intervention required
What Mine Management Gets
  • Predict blast vibration before each blast is fired — adjust charge if needed
  • Web app runs on any laptop — no specialist required to operate it
  • Every blast automatically logged to cloud (Google Sheets) — full audit trail
  • Model stays accurate as the mine moves to new seams — without IT support
  • Framework transfers to any Indian coal mine — only site constants change
Key Technical Finding for Mine Engineers
  • Total Charge (TQ) predicts PPV better than per-hole Scaled Distance across multi-seam operations — blast management should monitor total explosive energy, not just per-hole charge
  • Number of Holes is the top ML feature — the 60-year-old empirical formula ignores it entirely, causing systematic under-prediction for large blasts
  • Neural networks performed worst — ensemble tree methods are the right tool for mine-scale datasets
Next Step — IoT Automation
